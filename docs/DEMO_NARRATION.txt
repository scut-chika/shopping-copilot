DEMO VIDEO NARRATION — paste-ready for text-to-speech
=====================================================

Five blocks, about 3 minutes total at a normal reading pace.

HOW TO USE THIS FILE
  Paste ONE block at a time into your TTS tool. Do not paste the ==== header
  lines or the [ ] direction lines — those are for you, not the voice.
  Exact figures stay on screen in the terminal; the narration rounds them so
  they read naturally out loud.


==== BLOCK 1 — opening, about 20 seconds ====
[ on screen: the score table from tools/record.py ]

A customer says: I'm looking for tunics, but I'm still exploring. There are
fifty thousand products in the catalog. You have ten turns to put the one item
they actually want into your top ten.

The organizer's baseline manages that twelve percent of the time, and burns
almost all ten turns doing it. This system finds it every single time, in about
two turns, and ranks it first in one hundred ninety four sessions out of two
hundred.


==== BLOCK 2 — the live session, about 55 seconds ====
[ on screen: the turn-by-turn session. Pause on the one-item list. ]

Here is a real session. The agent has never seen the target.

Turn one. The customer has given it nothing but a category, so five hundred and
thirty four products are still consistent with what it knows. And watch what it
does not do. It does not fill ten slots to improve its odds. It shows one
product, its single best guess, and asks a question.

That is deliberate, and it is where most of the score comes from. The evaluator
ends the session the moment the target appears, at whatever rank it landed on.
Getting lucky on turn one at rank eight is not a win. It locks in rank eight
forever, and throws away the turn that would have made it rank one. Waiting one
more turn costs two percent of the efficiency score and buys up to thirty
percent of the ranking score. Thirteen to one.

Turn two. The customer answers, five hundred and thirty four candidates collapse
to three, and the product it puts up is the right one. Rank one, turn two.


==== BLOCK 3 — where the score comes from, about 40 seconds ====
[ on screen: the ablation table ]

We assumed the clever part would be the retrieval. It isn't.

Every customer sentence is built, word for word, from the target product's own
catalog record. So the right index is not a vector store. It is an inverted
index, from the thing the customer said back to the products that could have
said it.

But turn that off and the score barely moves. Turn off the question policy and a
third of the score disappears. Turn off the part that refuses to answer while
it is still guessing, and six more points go with it. Knowing what to ask, and
knowing when not to answer, is worth far more here than retrieving better.


==== BLOCK 4 — the honest part, about 45 seconds ====
[ on screen: the generalization table ]

A perfect hit rate on two hundred sessions is a warning sign, not a victory lap.
At the ceiling, that set cannot tell you anything anymore. You are just fitting
it.

So we built our own test. Eight hundred sessions, over products that appear
nowhere in the public set, with independently resampled users, at the official
scenario mix. Then we did it again with a second random seed.

Zero point nine three, both times, against zero point nine seven on the public
set.

So if you want one number for how this performs on data it has never seen, it is
zero point nine three, not zero point nine seven. We would rather tell you that
than quote the public figure and let it be read as a forecast. The repository
also names the one change that gained on the public set and gained nothing on
held out data. We kept it, and we do not count it.


==== BLOCK 5 — offline, about 25 seconds ====
[ on screen: the cost table, then the repo URL ]

The rules say final scoring may run with network access disabled. So the scored
configuration has no model in it. No API, no credentials, no third party
dependency, standard library only. Zero tokens, zero dollars, sixty six
milliseconds per turn.

There is an optional language model stage. It reads paraphrased customer
messages back onto catalog constraints, we measured it against a real model, and
it is switched off, because official scoring may cut the network.

And no model is not a claim in a README. One test starts a fresh interpreter and
fails if that module is so much as imported.

Shopping Copilot. Two turns instead of ten.
